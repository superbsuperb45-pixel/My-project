What a follow-up system actually needs

Automated lead follow-ups are only useful when they are specific, owned and measured. LeadRescue covers the four things spreadsheets miss.

A calendar you can trust

Every follow-up has a due date and time. The calendar shows the month at a glance and the day as a task list, so nothing depends on memory.

Overdue, not ignored

Overdue items rise to the top of the dashboard and the follow-up queue, and can automatically move the lead into an overdue status.

Templates with context

Email, WhatsApp and SMS templates insert the customer's name, company and deal value, so a follow-up reads like a message rather than a broadcast.

Outcomes, not checkboxes

Completing a follow-up requires the result. That data drives follow-up effectiveness, response rates and revenue per employee.

A follow-up sequence that survives a busy week

1. Same-day response

New inquiries are flagged if no first response is logged within your threshold. The lead stays critical until someone replies and records it.

2. Two to three working days

If there is no answer, the next follow-up is scheduled automatically with a different channel — call, then message.

3. Weekly decision point

Interested leads that stall past your threshold open a recovery flag with a concrete recommendation instead of quietly ageing.

4. Final attempt

Inactive leads get one deliberate last contact. If it produces nothing, close the lead as lost with a reason that feeds your reports.

5. Recover, convert or release

Recovered leads are marked separately from normal pipeline, and converted deals carry the revenue attributed to recovery.

6. Review the numbers

Weekly: overdue count, on-time rate, response rate and recovered revenue. Those four numbers tell you whether the process is working.
